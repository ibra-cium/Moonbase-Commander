# Impact: what kids learn, and how it helps NASA

> For the README, Video 2 and the judges. Team Space-ArenaX, NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026, Dhaka.
> Drafted 11 Nov with Claude (AI); the team checks every link before publishing.

## In one line

Moonbase Commander is junior astronaut training in a phone browser. Kids aged 7–13 run a Moon base through four short missions and make the same trade-offs NASA engineers make: air, water, power, radiation and crew rest. Every number comes from a NASA source.

## Who it is for

- Kids aged 7–13, at school or at home. One mission takes 3–15 minutes, so a class can play in one period.
- Free, one link, phone or laptop. **English and Bangla**, so kids in Bangladesh can learn space science in their own language.
- No accounts, no names, no data leaves the device.

## What they learn

| Mission | They do | They learn |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. First Landing | Pack air, water and food | An astronaut uses about 3.2 kg of water a day (NASA BVAD) |
| 2. Something Broke | Repair the water recycler | The space station recovers about 98 % of its water |
| 3. Solar Storm | Build a shelter from water or Moon soil | Water and regolith block radiation (storm dose 398 → 47 mSv) |
| 4. The Long Night | Keep power on through a 2-week night | Solar stops, batteries are too heavy, a small reactor works |

On top of that, kids collect **29 Moon Science Cards**. Each one is a real fact with its NASA (or partner) source, unlocked at the moment it matters: the sunset card when the sun sets, the 1972 storm card after surviving a storm, the ice card when the drill finds samples.

## How it helps NASA

1. **It does what NASA asked for.** The challenge asks for a game where students run an outpost and balance life support, shielding, power and food. NASA describes this year's lunar challenges as system-level models that simulate outpost operations and consider astronaut health and safety ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/nasa-welcomes-all-ages-experience-levels-for-space-apps-challenge/)). That is our game, built for kids.
2. **It shows kids NASA's real, open problems.** At the end of each mission a "NASA is working on this right now" card connects what the kid just did to a real NASA effort:
   - surviving the lunar night: the [Fission Surface Power](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/nasas-fission-surface-power-project-energizes-lunar-exploration) project and the [Watts on the Moon](https://www.nasa.gov/?p=734086) challenge
   - getting water on the Moon: the [Break the Ice](https://www.nasa.gov/prizes-challenges-and-crowdsourcing/centennial-challenges/break-the-ice-challenge) challenge
   - recycling: the [LunaRecycle](https://www.nasa.gov/prizes-challenges-and-crowdsourcing/centennial-challenges/lunarecycle/) challenge, where a student team won first prize in Phase 2
   - radiation: the [Helga and Zohar test dummies on Artemis I](https://www.dlr.de/en/research-and-transfer/featured-topics/mission-moon/artemis-programme/the-mare-cosmic-radiation-experiment-on-board-artemis-i)

   The message to kids: *these problems are not solved yet, and NASA asks the public for ideas.*
3. **It turns NASA open data into decisions a child can make.** Cargo limits, water use, storm doses, night length and reactor mass all come from NASA documents (see `data/sources.md`), and each one has a source tag in the game.
4. **It teaches a scientist's habit.** Every number and card has a "where does this come from?" tag, and kids can open the real source.

## How we know they learned (built in)

Every mission starts with one **"Guess first!"** question from Mission Control, before playing. The debrief asks the same question again after playing. The game counts both answers:

> Guessed right before playing: 1 / 4 → right after playing: 4 / 4

The map has a **Results** panel with a **Copy** button for teachers. It holds no names and no personal data.

**Playtest (fill in before Video 2):** we tested with ___ kids aged ___. Before: ___ / ___ correct. After: ___ / ___ correct. Science cards collected per kid: ___.
Rule for the test: no kids' names, faces or voices in any video or photo (hard rule 4). Ask a parent or teacher first.

## The judging criteria

Space Apps judges use five criteria: Impact, Creativity, Validity, Relevance and Presentation ([Project Submission Guide](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/resources/project-submission-guide/); the 2026 Judging and Awards Guide is released on 13 Nov, so check it then).

| Criterion | Our answer |
| --- | --- |
| Impact / influence | Kids 7–13, free, phone, Bangla + English, built-in before/after learning score |
| Creativity | A Clash-of-Clans-style mission map, Science Cards to collect, ranks from Cadet to Moonbase Commander |
| Validity | Every number from NASA sources, every card linked, simplifications labelled "Game assumption" |
| Relevance | Answers "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer" directly: run an outpost, balance life support, shielding, power and food |
| Presentation | Video shows: guess → play → storm → sunset → answer → NASA card → certificate |
